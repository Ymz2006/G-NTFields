Camera-follow planning

—

The camera's view is part of the shape

A camera follows a bird-like glider and must keep it in frame. The camera is a point; what it sees is a rectangle-based pyramid with its tip at the camera and its base pressed on the bird's back. Bird, camera and pyramid are planned as one rigid body in SE(3): a collision-free path means the bird flies, the camera flies, and nothing enters the line of sight. The same planners that move a shape through walls move this body; no camera-specific cost, no second planner.

How the test was run

Bodies and scenes: dataprocessing/make_camera3d.py. The bird is a polycube glider (fuselage, head, wings, tail plane, fin). The view pyramid's base is a 2-unit plate covering the bird's silhouette with a 2-unit margin; its tip is the camera, 50 units behind. The follower body is the same bird plus a 1-unit cube at the camera, so both bodies share one frame and one set of test pairs.

Forest: four trees (12-unit trunks to y = 96, 30-unit crowns at y 90..120) in the room of the shape cells. City: six buildings 45..100 units high in two rows, streets 40, 56 and 60 wide. With the view pyramid 3.5% (forest) and 4.5% (city) of uniformly random placements are collision-free, against 4.3% for the same body in env9.

Training: 400k narrow-band pairs per cell (sharded preprocessing with an exact signed-distance gate, preprocess_obj_fast.py), 8000 epochs, seeds 0-2, for the lifted-encoder head (graph_homotopy_lift), the homotopy head (graph_homotopy, --lift min) and the full metric NTFields (TD and normal terms 1e-3). Test sets: 1000 RRT-verified pairs of the camera-aware body per scene (make_test_sets.py --variant std).

Scoring (plan_eval_homotopy.py --camera): the same MPPI planner and collision test as every other comparison (50k scene surface points inside the placed body); in addition, at every waypoint the camera casts a ray to each of its visible bird surface samples against the scene mesh (visible = not hidden by the bird itself), and the bird alone is collision-checked. The scene views above draw the plan up to SE(3) convergence, the bird's path, the camera's path, the body at the start, the end and the frame where the camera first loses the bird or the body first collides.
