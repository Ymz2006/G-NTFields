Drone chase camera

—

The camera's field of view is part of the shape

A small delta-wing drone is followed by a chase camera on its axis. The camera is a point; what it sees up to the drone is a rectangle-based pyramid with its tip at the camera and its base on the drone's back. Drone, camera and pyramid are planned as one rigid body in SE(3): a collision-free path means the drone flies, the camera flies, and nothing enters the shot. The same planners that move a shape through a room move this body; no camera-specific cost, no second planner. Six set-ups: the camera 30, 55 or 80 units behind the drone (1.5, 2.75 or 4 wing spans), each with a tight or a wide field of view.

How the test was run

Bodies and scenes: dataprocessing/make_dronecam3d.py. The drone is a polycube delta wing (fuselage 14 × 4 × 4, wing span 20, fin 4 tall). The view pyramid's base is a 2-unit plate on the drone's back, 22 × 10 (tight, the silhouette plus a 1-unit margin) or 34 × 16 (wide); its tip is the camera 30, 55 or 80 units behind. The follower body is the same drone plus a 1-unit cube at the camera, so both bodies of a distance share one frame and one set of test pairs.

Five scenes in the room of the shape cells, easiest to hardest: meadow (five trees, two sheds), suburb (six houses along two streets, eight trees whose 22–30-unit branches overhang the streets), campus (six blocks 64–95 high, three sky bridges, six trees, porticos), downtown (a 3 × 3 grid of towers 84–130 high with streets 40 and 44 wide, four sky bridges, masts, porticos, four street trees) and overgrown (the tower grid with eight kerb trees whose branches cross the streets, three cables, three sky bridges). Trees have a 6 × 6 trunk, a stepped crown and two to four 4 × 4 branches with a leaf clump: thin, high things the drone clears easily and a camera 30–80 units behind it does not.

Training: 400k narrow-band pairs per cell (sharded preprocessing on signed-distance grids with exact fallback, preprocess_obj_fast.py), 4000 epochs, seeds 0–2, for the lifted-encoder head (graph_homotopy_lift) and the full metric NTFields (TD and normal terms 1e-3). Test sets: 1000 RRT-verified pairs of the camera-aware body per scene and camera set-up (make_test_sets.py --variant std).

Scoring (plan_eval_homotopy.py --camera): the same planner and collision test as every other comparison (50k scene surface points inside the placed body); in addition, at every waypoint the camera casts a ray to each of its visible drone surface samples against the scene mesh, and the drone alone is collision-checked. The scene views draw the plan up to SE(3) convergence, the drone's path, the camera's path, the body at the start, the end and the frame where the camera first loses the drone or the body first collides; the camera views are the field of view itself at six waypoints.
